Living in Florida

Plan for More Than the Price of a Home

Housing is only one part of a Florida household budget. Insurance, utilities, transportation, taxes, and everyday expenses can all affect the true cost of relocating.

Understanding the Cost of Living in Florida

The cost of living can vary considerably depending on where you choose to live in Florida. A household in a large coastal metropolitan area may face different expenses than one in an inland or smaller community.

When comparing locations, it is helpful to look beyond a home's purchase price or monthly rent. Consider the complete household budget and research the expenses that are most important to your lifestyle.

Housing

Housing is often one of the largest expenses associated with relocating. Purchase prices and rents can vary widely between coastal cities, major metropolitan areas, suburbs, and smaller inland communities.

Compare neighborhoods as well as cities when researching housing.

Insurance

Insurance deserves special attention when planning a move to Florida. Homeowners, renters, automobile, flood, and other insurance needs can vary depending on the property, location, and individual circumstances.

Obtain insurance estimates before committing to a home.

Utilities

Electricity, water, internet, waste services, and other utilities should be included in a monthly budget. Florida's warm climate can also make air-conditioning an important part of household energy use.

Ask about typical utility costs when comparing homes.

Transportation

Transportation costs depend heavily on where you live and work. Fuel, vehicle maintenance, insurance, parking, and toll roads may all become part of a household's regular transportation expenses.

Consider both housing cost and commute cost together.

Taxes

Taxes are another part of the overall cost of living. Property taxes, sales taxes, and other local costs can differ by community and should be considered when comparing possible locations.

Research the taxes that apply to your specific situation.

Everyday Expenses

Groceries, dining, child care, recreation, health care, and other routine expenses can affect whether a community fits comfortably within your budget.

Build a realistic monthly budget before making your move.

Relocation Planning

Look at the Complete Monthly Picture

A lower home price does not automatically mean a lower overall cost of living. A longer commute, different insurance costs, higher utility use, or other recurring expenses can change the financial picture.

Before choosing a community, create a sample monthly budget using realistic estimates for housing, insurance, transportation, utilities, food, health care, savings, and recreation. Comparing the complete budget can provide a clearer picture than comparing housing prices alone.

Research Before You Relocate

Costs change over time and can vary significantly from one household and community to another. Use current information from housing providers, insurance companies, utility providers, local governments, and other reliable sources when developing your relocation budget.
