Getting Around Florida

Transportation & Toll Roads

Transportation can have a major impact on where you choose to live in Florida. Commute times, toll roads, traffic, parking, and access to major highways should all be considered when comparing communities.

Before You Move

Plan More Than the Distance

Two communities may appear close together on a map, but the daily driving experience can be very different. Traffic patterns, toll roads, bridges, tourist areas, and the location of major employment centers can all affect a commute.

When researching a possible home, consider testing the route between the neighborhood and the places you expect to visit regularly during the times you would normally travel.

SunPass

SunPass is Florida's prepaid toll program. Drivers can use a compatible transponder to pay tolls electronically on participating toll roads and bridges.

Toll Roads

Toll roads are common in several parts of Florida, particularly around major metropolitan areas. Regular toll expenses should be considered when estimating the true cost of a daily commute.

Major Highways

Interstate 4, Interstate 75, Interstate 95, Florida's Turnpike, and other major routes connect communities throughout the state. Access to these roads can strongly influence travel times.

Public Transportation

Public transportation options vary significantly by region. Some metropolitan areas offer bus or rail service, while many communities are primarily designed around automobile travel.

Florida Resource

Check Current Toll Information

Toll rates, road conditions, and payment options can change. Before traveling or planning a regular commute, check current information from an official source.

Visit the Official SunPass Website

Test Your Future Commute

If possible, drive your potential commute during normal rush hour conditions before choosing a home. A neighborhood that looks perfect on a map may feel very different after experiencing the daily route firsthand.
